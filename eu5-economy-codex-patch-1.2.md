# EU5 Economy Codex — Patch 1.2 (Echinades)

Source: [EU5 1.2 Economy Guide! 9 Things You're Doing Wrong](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rZd40Q2-RUo)

---

## 1. The Wealth / Tax Base Trap

Patch 1.2 introduced **national wealth**: as your country grows richer and more populous, your economic costs rise. If your **tax base** (how efficiently you collect taxes) falls behind wealth, you become net poorer despite generating more income.

- Uncollected taxes flow into estate wealth
- Once an estate exceeds **25% power**, each additional percent cuts your tax collection from that estate by 1%
- This creates a self-perpetuating death spiral if ignored

**Fix:** Close the gap by increasing control everywhere you build.

---

## 2. Control Is Everything

Control is the core lever of the patch 1.2 economy.

**Sources of proximity (≈ control):**
- Capital: 100 proximity points by default
- Local governors (2 at start, up to ~5–6 with tech)
- Maritime governors (requires government reforms, maxed out)
- Bailiffs in villages (+20% control)
- Towns, cities, megalopolises themselves
- Era 2+: control buildings in towns/cities
- Roads, bridges, canals — extend proximity spread
- River networks — natural proximity corridors
- Fleet patrols — increase naval presence → reduce coastal proximity costs
- Harbour buildings — increase harbour capacity → reduce proximity costs at sea

**Hard rule: build nothing below 50% control.** Low-control buildings increase wealth without increasing your tax base, making you poorer.

---

## 3. Production Buildings — Where to Build

Production buildings (burghers, laborers, clergy, peasants categories) generate income via taxation of their output.

- Always build first in your **capital** (100% control = full tax base)
- Max them out before expanding outward
- Then move to highest-control locations (local governor seats)
- Each building level = **+1% production efficiency**
- Production efficiency causes buildings to produce ~20% more output from the same inputs — maximising profit per location

**In patch 1.2, capitalist economy is always worth pursuing.** Traditional economy no longer competes. In patch 1.1 you could debate it; not anymore.

**Specialisation via Borrow Privileges (Era 3, printing press tree):**
- Boost production efficiency for a specific good type
- But reduce overall production efficiency for all buildings
- **Do not stack more than one** — three privileges = −15% efficiency, wiping all gains
- Each location now shows which goods it produces most efficiently

---

## 4. RGOs — Don't Spam Blindly

Pre-1.2 rule "spam RGOs if you don't know what to spend on" **no longer works.**

- Expensive RGOs (iron, silver, gold) massively increase wealth...
- ...but in low-control areas, generate almost no tax income
- The wealth increase raises your costs; the taxes don't compensate

**Rules for RGOs:**
- Only build in high-control locations (use construction planner, filter ≥50% control)
- In remote areas: deliberately **lower market access** — reduces wealth generation while still supplying raw materials
- Build bailiffs in remote areas to partially offset low control

---

## 5. Service Buildings — Build Everywhere

Service buildings (temples, armories, bridges, theaters, universities, markets) don't directly generate income, but:

- You pay only **20% of their upkeep** — the market covers the rest
- More service buildings → higher demand for maintenance goods → goods prices rise → your tax income from production buildings increases
- Upper-class workers (required by service buildings) consume more goods → further price increases

**Build these across all locations regardless of control level.** The market-driven price effect benefits your high-control production buildings.

---

## 6. Trade — Break Even, Don't Chase Profit

Direct trade profit is weak vs. taxation. But trade is still useful via the **trade orders** mechanic (new in 1.2):

- Trade orders direct sale of specific goods → raises their market price
- Higher goods prices → your production buildings pay more taxes
- Goal: use trade orders to inflate prices on goods you produce in high-control areas

**To actually profit from trade, two conditions required:**
1. **Cheaper goods than competitors** — achieved via production efficiency (capitalist economy)
2. **Low trade transport costs** — achieved via:
   - Banking System government reform (requires 50% capitalist economy, −40% merchant costs)
   - Naval presence (fleet patrol + harbour buildings, new in 1.2: also reduces sea transport costs — patch notes only, not shown in tooltips)
   - Merchant republics start with up to 90% transport cost reduction; others unlock more post-Reformation

**Trade range (1.2 redesign):** limits are now purely based on profitability. Trade range tech increases the distance you can trade before distance penalties kick in.

---

## 7. Navy — More Than Combat

From patch 1.2, **naval presence reduces sea transport costs for trade** (undocumented in-game, patch notes only).

**Building the navy economy:**
- Patrol fleets → naval presence → reduce coastal proximity costs AND sea trade transport costs
- Build harbour-capacity buildings → increases naval presence effectiveness
- Send fleets to trade outpost locations to boost naval presence there
- Higher naval presence → better market standing → trade priority in that market

---

## 8. Population & Workers

**Patch 1.2 population change:** All social classes now reproduce independently (previously only peasants reproduced and promoted upward). The old promotion system still exists but at 1/10th the rate.

**Key actions:**
- Unlock **Social Ladder** technology (Era 2, banking tree) — cabinet action that accelerates social promotion into higher classes
- Don't convert every village to towns/cities — village population growth is naturally highest
- Mixed province setup (at least one city/town + villages) + large food reserves + urban privilege = significant population growth boost

---

## 9. Serfdom vs. Free Subjects

The choice is more complex in 1.2.

| | Serfdom | Free Subjects |
|---|---|---|
| **Best for** | Large nations, warmongers, low-control empires | Small/medium nations, high-control, peaceful |
| **Military** | +25% levies, 50% faster replenishment | −25% levies (net −50% swing if switching) |
| **Economy** | Nobility taxation caps ~50–60% | Peasant taxation can reach 100% |
| **Peasant infranchisement** | Lower | Higher (especially in high-control locations) |

**General rule:**
- Start with whichever value you already have more of at game start
- Large warring nation → serfdom
- High-control small nation (e.g. Bohemia) → free subjects economically superior
- Switch to free subjects after ~1450 once you have at least one standing army

Exceptions: Japanese Shogunate, France — strong results with nobility under serfdom.

---

## 10. Megalopolis (New in 1.2)

The highest location tier. Build in your capital as a priority.

**Bonuses:**
- 4,000 workers + 4,000 burghers by default
- 50% faster building construction

**Requirements:**
- 7,000–8,000 gold
- 400,000 population
- Only one per empire (unless you conquer cities that already have one)

---

## Summary: Priority Order

1. **Max control** near capital before building outward
2. **Go capitalist economy** — production efficiency is king in 1.2
3. **Build production buildings** in capital first, max level, then next-highest control locations
4. **Service buildings everywhere** — let market price effects do the work
5. **No buildings below 50% control** (except service buildings)
6. **Trade orders** to inflate goods prices, not for direct trade profit
7. **Fleet patrols** for naval presence (control spread + trade costs)
8. **Megalopolis** in capital ASAP
9. **Serfdom early, consider free subjects post-1450**
